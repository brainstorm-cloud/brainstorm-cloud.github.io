Investing Crash Course — the straight version

~15 minutes · no hype · no picks · just how it works

1 · How the stock market actually works

A share is a tiny slice of a company. Buy one share, you own a sliver of that business — its profits, its debts, its future.

The exchange is the marketplace where slices get bought and sold — the NYSE and Nasdaq are the two big ones in the US. It's an auction: millions of buyers and sellers shouting prices at each other, matched in milliseconds.

The broker is your doorway in. You don't walk onto the exchange floor — you open an account with a broker (an app on your phone), deposit money, and tap buy or sell. The broker routes your order to the exchange.

What moves prices: supply and demand, nothing more mysterious. More buyers than sellers → price goes up. More sellers than buyers → down. What shifts the balance? Company earnings, news, interest rates, the economy, and — honestly — crowd emotion. Fear and greed move markets every single day.

That's the whole machine: slices, auction, doorway, crowd. Everything else is decoration.

2 · How crypto actually works

Bitcoin is digital money with no bank behind it. Instead of a bank's ledger, thousands of computers around the world each keep the same copy of the record — that's the blockchain. No one can rewrite it because everyone would have to agree to the lie.

A wallet is where you hold it. It's not a wallet like leather — it's a secret key (a long password). Whoever has the key owns the coins. Lose the key, lose the coins. No password-reset button. No customer service.

Exchanges are the crypto version of brokers — apps where you swap dollars for coins. Same auction idea as stocks, except it never closes: 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

What moves prices: the same supply and demand — but crypto has far fewer buyers and sellers than stocks, so prices swing violently. A single rumor can move Bitcoin 10% in a day. Smaller coins can drop 90% and never come back. There are no earnings reports or real businesses behind most of them — just belief, hype, and speculation.

Know this: Bitcoin is the only crypto with a 15+ year track record. Thousands of other coins have gone to effectively zero. Most "new coins" are built to enrich the people who made them, not you.

3 · The honest math

Here's what $60 actually does. The US stock market has returned about 10% a year on average over the long run (before inflation). Watch what that means:

$60 at 10%/year:
· after 1 year → $66
· after 10 years → ~$156
· after 30 years → ~$1,047

To turn $60 into $1,000,000 at 10% a year takes over 100 years. That's not pessimism — that's arithmetic. Anyone promising faster is selling you something.

Why day-trading small accounts usually loses

  • The math of smallness: a 10% win on $60 is $6. One bad trade wipes out ten good ones. Fees and spreads eat the rest.
  • The pros you're against: you're trading against firms with supercomputers that react in microseconds. Your phone tap is not faster.
  • The evidence: a major study of day traders found roughly 97% lost money. Regulators warn about it openly. This isn't a skill gap you close in a weekend.
  • The $25,000 rule: in the US, you legally need $25,000 in a margin account to day-trade stocks freely. With $60, the rules themselves fence you out.
  • The emotions: losing real money makes smart people do dumb things — chase losses, double down, panic-sell. Paper trading can't teach you this; only small real losses can, and they're tuition, not income.

4 · The biggest beginner traps

🪝 Leverage ("10x your money!") — borrowed money that multiplies wins AND losses. A 10% drop with 10x leverage = your entire account gone. Exchanges love offering it because your liquidation is their profit.
🪝 Meme coins — coins with a dog on them and no purpose. They pump when influencers shill them and crash when those same people sell. You are the exit liquidity — the person they sell to.
🪝 "Gurus" selling courses — if someone's trading system reliably made millions, they'd be trading, not selling you a $499 course. Their income is the course, not the market.
🪝 Pump groups — chat groups that "pump" a coin at a set time. The organizers buy early, announce late, and sell into the buying frenzy they created. You buy the top.
🪝 Fake exchanges & DMs — anyone who DMs you about investing, "doubling your crypto," or a can't-miss platform is running a scam. Real opportunities never arrive by DM. Never send coins to "verify" or "unlock" anything.

One rule catches nearly all of them: if you don't understand where the profit comes from, you ARE the profit.

5 · Paper trading — learn free

Paper trading = fake money, real markets. You get a pretend account loaded with virtual cash and trade live prices. You learn how orders work, how charts read, how fast money can vanish — without losing a cent. Every beginner should do months of this before touching real money.

Two real free options

  • Webull paper trading — free, on your phone, up to $1M in virtual cash with unlimited resets. Stocks, ETFs, options. Good for learning how a real broker app feels.
  • TradingView paper trading — free on every plan including the free tier, right on the charts. Customizable virtual balance, covers stocks, crypto, and more. Best for learning to read price charts.

Paper trading teaches mechanics, not emotions. You'll still need to learn the fear-and-greed part later — with money you can afford to lose.

6 · The real path

Step 1 — Earn first. $60 doesn't compound into wealth; skills do. The phone-service business, the apps, bug bounties — that's the money engine. Investing multiplies money you already have. No money, nothing to multiply.
Step 2 — Park profits in boring stuff. When income lands, the unsexy winners are index funds — one purchase that owns a slice of hundreds of companies at once (like the whole S&P 500). Low fees, no stock-picking, ~10%/year historically. Boring is the point.
Step 3 — Long holds over gambling. Time in the market beats timing the market. Money you won't need for 5+ years can ride out crashes. Money you need next month doesn't belong invested at all.
Step 4 — Keep learning, keep paper trading. The market will still be there in a year. An educated $6,000 beats a lucky $60 every time — because luck runs out and knowledge compounds.

Nobody in this document is telling you what to buy. That's deliberate. The day you can explain WHY something is worth owning — in your own words — is the day you're ready.
